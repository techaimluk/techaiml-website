Watching every cow for signs of heat or illness is hard on a busy farm, especially in large or loose-housed herds. Rumen boluses offer another way: a small sensor that lives inside the animal and reports around the clock.

## How a rumen bolus works

A bolus is given by mouth and settles in the reticulorumen, the first compartments of the cow's stomach, where it stays. Sensors inside it log core temperature and movement, and the readings are sent wirelessly to a receiver on the farm. Software then compares each cow with her own normal pattern and flags anything unusual.

## What the readings reveal

Temperature is the headline measurement. A 2020 study of 190 heat events found that rumen temperature rises by around half a degree during oestrus, with larger rises in cows showing stronger heat behaviour. The same study found that temperature alone was not a precise heat alert, which is why combining it with activity data matters.

Drinking also leaves a clear trace. A drink of cool water causes a sharp, short drop in rumen temperature, so boluses can estimate how often and how much a cow drinks. Unexplained temperature rises can point to fever or infection, and changes before calving can help predict its timing.

## Why it matters

Earlier, more reliable heat detection means fewer missed services and shorter calving intervals. On one farm in the EU-funded 4D4F project, introducing boluses cut days open from 148 to 123. Spotting illness early also helps welfare and can reduce treatment costs.
